# Marketing plan

The product's growth loop is built in: every session and every player page renders as a card in WhatsApp, and every card carries a start-your-own-crew action. Marketing exists to put the first cards into the right group chats and to make the season-end content people want to post. Paid spend is gated on retention, not on ambition.

## Who we are talking to

**The organiser**, not the player. One person per crew books the pitch or court, fronts the money and chases the group. They are 22 to 35, London, in a WhatsApp group of 8 to 20 that has existed for years. If we win the organiser we win the crew; the members never need to be marketed to because the invite link does it.

Where organisers already are: Powerleague and Goals league nights, Rocket and Padium and Padel Social Club peak slots, Footy Addicts regulars who want their own group, work teams, university alumni groups, run clubs that also play five-a-side.

## Positioning

"The app for your crew, not your sport." One line under it depending on the audience: "Who's actually turning up?" for organisers, "Late drops still pay" for the money-chasers, "Sick Note leaderboard" for the banter crowd.

Never say "social sports platform", "community" or "fitness". Say crew, mates, turn up, sick note, bibs, court, tee.

## Stage 1, months 0 to 4: fifteen crews, no paid spend

Goal: prove the loop retains. Gate to Stage 2: at least 11 of the 15 pilot crews still pinning sessions unprompted at week 12.

- **Recruit in person.** Two evenings a week at Powerleague Shoreditch, Goals Wembley, Rocket Battersea, Padium Canary Wharf. Find the person collecting cash. Set their crew up on the spot, on their phone, before they leave. Target mix: 6 football, 4 padel, 2 golf, 2 gym, 1 race weekend.
- **Concierge the first four weeks.** Message every organiser the day after each session. Fix what they hit. Log every reason a session did not get pinned.
- **Share-card seeding.** Ask each pilot organiser to post the recap card after their first played session. Track how many new crews come from each posted card (the card carries a referral code in its link).
- **Content from the pilot only.** Weekly Sick Note leaderboard, player card reveals, "worst miss of the week" voted by the crew. Posted from the brand account on TikTok and Instagram with the crew's permission. No stock, no explainers.
- **Founder-led.** The founder replies to every organiser personally. This is not scalable and is not meant to be.

Budget: £0 paid. Costs are pitch fees for demo sessions and a few hundred pounds of printed cards for venue noticeboards.

## Stage 2, months 5 to 10: London, £150k paid

Gate to Stage 3: 300 active London crews and customer acquisition cost paid back within 12 months from payments margin plus venue revenue. Both are assumptions until the pilot produces numbers.

- **Paid social.** TikTok and Instagram Reels, Meta interest and lookalike audiences seeded from pilot organisers. Creative is the product output: real cards, real tables, real Sick Note counts. Test against organiser pain lines: "£65 pitch, three dropouts, you're paying", "It's 7pm, who's actually coming?". Budget assumption £90k.
- **Venue co-marketing.** Offer venues a reliable-crew badge and standing-slot priority for crews with a 90 per cent turn-up rate. Venue puts our QR on the pitch-side board and in booking confirmations. First targets: Powerleague, Goals, Rocket Padel, Padium, Padel Social Club, Game4Padel. These are targets, not partners. Budget assumption £20k in venue incentives.
- **Creators.** Ten London football and padel creators paid to run their own crew on the app for a season, not to advertise it. Content is their table and their cards. Budget assumption £30k.
- **University and workplace leagues.** Freshers' fairs in September, corporate five-a-side leagues year round. Organisers here run several crews at once.
- **PR.** One story: "the app that makes late drops pay". Pitch to The Athletic UK, Padel Nation, Bunkered, Men's Health UK, and London freesheets.
- **Referral mechanic.** Any crew that spawns three active crews gets a season-end awards pack (printed cards, engraved Sick Note trophy).

## Stage 3, months 11 to 18: four cities, £200k paid

Manchester, Birmingham, Bristol, Leeds, in that order, using the London playbook: in-person venue recruitment for the first 15 crews per city, then paid social seeded from local organisers. Hire a community and venues lead per region.

## Channels we will not use

- **Player-finder listings.** Strangers do not retain. The crew is the unit.
- **App store ads.** The product is web-first; the install is the invite link.
- **Discount codes and cash incentives to members.** The organiser is the customer; paying members to join distorts the reliability signal the whole product is built on.
- **Paid acquisition before the Stage 1 gate.** Spending to acquire crews that leave in four weeks proves nothing and burns the round.

## Measurement

| Metric | Definition | Stage 1 target |
|---|---|---|
| Crews activated | Crew with one session pinned and at least four people tapped in | 15 |
| Week-12 retention | Crews still pinning without a prompt | 11 of 15 |
| Cards posted | Recap or player cards shared from the app per crew per month | 3 |
| Referral rate | New crews created from a shared card, per 100 cards posted | 5 |
| Cost per activated crew | Paid spend divided by activated crews, Stage 2 onwards | under £60, assumption |

## Content calendar, weekly

- Monday: Sick Note leaderboard across all crews (anonymised first names).
- Wednesday: player card reveal, one crew.
- Friday: "This week's worst miss", crew-voted, with the crew's blessing.
- Sunday: race-weekend podium predictor results during F1 weekends.
- Monthly: crew of the month, awards pack in the post.
